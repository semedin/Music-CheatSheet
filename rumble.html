01

What a rumble actually is

A classic rumble is a kick’s processed echo, shaped into low-end movement. But a played sub, a delayed tom or a chopped texture can do the same job. Reverb resonances and distortion can change the perceived pitch: tune the source, then listen to the finished tail. The diagram shows the classic parallel version.

Job 1A focused kick

A stable kick body can give the low end a tonal anchor. Start around a third to half a beat in length, then shorten or extend it by ear to leave room for the tail.

Job 2A useful tail

Reverb stretches the source; drive adds harmonics that can help it translate. Filters shape its role, but low-mid buildup can still need a targeted cut or a quieter tail.

Job 3The pump

Sidechain carves space for the kick transient, then lets the tail swell back in. The swell between kicks is the groove. How fast it returns sets the feel, from floaty to galloping.

One track. A whole low-end instrument.

Yes: multiple instruments, parallel effects, independent levels and shared tuning can live inside one rack. Choose the structure that fits how you want to play.

The quickest useful answer

One kick.
Two paths.
Eight good knobs.

Start with Simpler → Audio Effect Rack. One dry chain keeps the punch; one wet chain makes the rumble. Tune Simpler once and both paths receive that source.

Want multiple instruments too? Use an Instrument Rack. Want the whole thing under one drum pad? Nest that rack inside a Drum Rack.

MIDI → Simpler (your kick) ↓ Audio Effect Rack ├─ DRY → Utility └─ WET → Reverb 100% → Saturator → EQ → Compressor → Utility ↓ one track output

The wet branch must not leak a second dry transient. Add any final output control after the rack.

Make one tuning knob work properly.

  1. Calibrate first

    Different samples at Transpose 0 are not necessarily the same pitch. Tune their settled bodies individually to one target. Match synth octaves by frequency too: DAWs differ in octave labels.

  2. Then map a relative transpose

    In a single-source rack, tune the source. In a layered Instrument Rack, put a MIDI Pitch device at the start of each instrument chain and map them to the same macro, or map each instrument’s transpose with ranges that preserve your calibrated offsets. Use −12 to +12 semitones as a starting span.

  3. Keep drum-pad selection separate

    Pitch before a Drum Rack changes which pad gets triggered. Put pitch changes inside the target pad, or map its instruments’ transpose controls. The pad’s Receive note selects the pad; its Play note is sent to its instrument.

Sidechain without another track.

On the wet chain’s Compressor, enable external Sidechain. Select this MIDI track, then the dry kick chain’s audio tap in the lower chooser. Use the tap after the instrument, before rumble processing. Verify that only the dry kick drives the meter.

At track level, Pre FX on a MIDI track is not the synthesized kick audio. Select the rack chain tap, or the appropriate Post FX signal on a separate clean kick track. Never use the complete kick-plus-rumble output as the trigger.

If nested routing makes the desired tap unavailable, draw a repeating gain envelope on a Utility inside the wet branch. This needs no audio detector. Align it with the actual kick pattern; a free-running quarter-note shape will still dip when a kick is missing.

Quick test: turn the wet branch down, play the kick, and confirm the Compressor’s gain-reduction meter still moves. Then bring the wet branch back up.

A macro panel you will actually use

Suggested mappings, not a supplied .adg rack. Start with narrow ranges; level-match the extremes.

01 / TUNE

Relative pitch ±12 st. Calibrate layers first; leave unpitched clicks alone.

02 / WEIGHT

Wet-chain level. Start −24 to −6 dB; judge against the kick.

03 / ROOM

Reverb decay 0.6–4 s. Start short for fast or busy patterns.

04 / DIRT

Saturator Drive 0–9 dB; map Output downward to roughly compensate.

05 / SHADE

Low-pass 140–700 Hz. Brighter means more texture, not more sub.

06 / BREATHE

Compressor Release 60–320 ms. Confirm recovery at your BPM.

07 / SPACE

Pre-delay 0–30 ms. This separates onset; it is not ducking.

08 / GHOST

High-passed texture-chain level. Keep its deep lows out.

Save three Macro Variations: Tight / Rolling / Destroyed. Exclude Tune and output level from variation changes if you want to preserve them. Save the outer rack to your User Library; save any sample files it depends on with your project.

Creative constraint / ten minutes

Make the silence heavier than the sound.

Use only two rumble events per bar. Give them different lengths. Keep the kick unchanged, and make the second bar answer the first.

Choose who owns the bottom.

Kick-led: short sub-heavy kick, rumble high-passed a little higher. Rumble-led: tighter kick, fuller tail. Separate bassline: make the rumble a quiet low-mid texture or remove it when the bass speaks.

A lower high-pass is not automatically heavier. Two layers fighting at the same frequency can sound smaller together.

Listen for a useful difference.

Compare at similar loudness. Listen to eight bars, not one hit. Does the tail add pace, space, menace or bounce? Mute it: if the groove improves, simplify its timing before making it louder.

For phase checks, compare in mono and try polarity inversion on one layer. Neither setting is universally correct, and reverb phase moves over time.

04

Key & tuning

43–62 Hz (roughly F1 to B1 in this page’s octave notation) is a useful starting window for a low kick, not a rule. Lower or higher kicks can work. Choose register for the sound and playback system, then check the relationship with your bass and harmony.

Your track key → kick options

Uses the transport key. “Near 50 Hz” is only a register hint; choose the note by ear against your music. Note labels here use C4 = middle C; compare Hz if your DAW labels octaves differently.

DegreeNoteHzVerdict

The rules that matter

  1. Tune the tail, not the click

    A kick sweeps down in pitch. Its perceived note is where the sweep lands. Measure the body after the first ~50 ms in Spectrum, not the transient.

  2. Root is a useful first comparison

    A tonal rumble on the root often supports a static harmony. It does not fit every chord or bass note automatically. Short, noisy kicks may have no stable pitch worth forcing to a note.

  3. Compare a fifth and another octave

    For C or D, roots around 33/37 Hz or 65/73 Hz offer different weight. G or A may suit the arrangement too. Choose the harmony and punch you want rather than automatically preferring the closest note to 50 Hz.

  4. Kick first is valid too

    Many techno producers find a kick they love, tune it by ear, then write the track in that key. Either direction works as long as they agree.

  5. Check changing harmony

    A sustained tonal tail can clash when chords move. Shorten it, reduce its tonal emphasis, choose a shared pedal note, or use the MIDI-driven rumble in Recipe C to follow the harmony.

NoteHzCharacter as a rumble fundamentalGood for
Your A minor track at 130 BPM: A1 = 55 Hz is right in the window. Deep, punchy, translates well. The fifth, E1 = 41 Hz, is heavier on a big system but can vanish on laptops and earbuds.
05

Length & timing

Everything here is relative to one beat. The kick body takes the first third to half of it; the rumble owns the rest; the pump decides where the handover happens. The highlighted row follows the transport BPM.

BPM1 beat1/161/32 (pre-delay)Kick bodyPump releaseReverb decay

How long should the kick be?

StyleKick bodyWhy
Hypnotic / deep220–300 msRound body, rumble fades in behind it
Peak-time warehouse180–250 msPunch first, rumble does the bass work
Rolling / grumble160–220 msShort so the 16th movement is audible
Hard techno 145+120–180 msBeats are short, the tail needs space
Dub / melodic250–350 msSofter, more sub; the wash stays quieter

A Splice sample that rings 600 ms+ is a boom kick, not a rumble kick body. Shorten it with a fade in Simpler or pick a tighter one.

Three timing rules

  1. The tail must peak before the next kick

    If the pump is still recovering when the next kick hits, the rumble never breathes and the low end sounds flat. Release around half a beat is the starting point.

  2. Reverb decay longer than you think

    1.5–4.5 s sounds absurd soloed, but the pump chops it every beat. Long decay means a steady, even rumble; short decay means a bouncing one.

  3. Pre-delay separates, it doesn't delay

    5–25 ms (around a 1/64–1/32 note) keeps the reverb onset out of the kick transient. Beyond ~40 ms you start hearing a flam.

06

Ableton Live 12

Three workflows to complement the one-track rack builds: classic sends, resampling, and played sub notes. B lets you commit and edit audio; C gives you a tonal layer that can follow harmony. Device availability varies by Live edition.

Recipe A: the classic send rumble

  1. Put the kick on its own track and tune it

    Load the kick into Simpler’s One-Shot mode. Add Spectrum after it, inspect the settled low body, and adjust Transpose/Detune if a pitch change improves its fit with your track.

    Simpler One-Shot, Warp offSpectrum Block 8192, Avg 1Target 43–62 Hz

  2. Make a Return track called RUMBLE

    Send the kick to it. Alternative: duplicate the kick track and process the copy 100% wet. The duplicate lets you mute individual rumble hits without touching the kick.

    Send A −6 dB to 0 dBReturn Post

  3. Hybrid Reverb, fully wet

    Dark, large, long. Make sure nothing in the reverb's own EQ is cutting the lows, because the lows are the point.

    Dry/Wet 100%Pre-delay 10–20 msDecay 2–4 sSize largeAlgorithm a dark hallReverb low cut off

    Classic Reverb works too: Decay 3 s, Diffusion high, Hi filter around 2 kHz, Dry/Wet 100%.

  4. Saturate before you filter

    Distortion creates harmonics that make the rumble audible on small speakers. Filtering afterwards keeps those harmonics dark and low.

    Saturator Bass Shaper or Analog ClipDrive 6–15 dBSoft Clip onor Roar single stage, amount 30–60%

  5. EQ Eight: box it in

    High-pass the subsonic mud, low-pass everything above the low mids.

    Band 1 HP 48 dB @ 30–35 HzBand 8 LP 48 dB @ 180–300 HzOptional −2 dB bell at kick fundamental

  6. Compressor with sidechain from the kick

    This is the pump. Aim for deep gain reduction and a release that recovers just before the next kick.

    Sidechain clean kick audio: Post FX after Simpler, or its rack chain tapRatio start 6:1Attack 1–3 msRelease 100–250 msThreshold adjust for space around the kick

    Tighter alternative: Auto Pan-Tremolo in its shape/tremolo mode, synced to 1/4, with a ramp-up curve phase-aligned to the kick. No trigger lag at all.

  7. Utility: mono the lows

    Stereo reverb puts phase movement into the sub. Collapse it.

    Utility Bass Mono onFreq 120 Hzor Width 0%

  8. Balance and check

    Loop 8 bars, set levels by ear, then check mono, a small speaker and a reference track.

    Rumble peak 4–10 dB under kickSpectrum no build-up at 250–400 Hz

Recipe B: resample and chop

  1. Build Recipe A first

    Get the rumble sounding right in context.

  2. Record the Return

    New audio track, Audio From: RUMBLE return (Post FX), Monitor Off. Record 8 bars while the kick loop plays.

    Audio From A-ReturnMonitor Off

  3. Cut a steady-state loop

    Skip bar 1 (the tail isn't built up yet). Select bars 3–4, Consolidate (Ctrl/Cmd+J).

  4. Now edit it like a sample

    Clip envelopes for volume per beat, transpose per bar to follow chords, reverse a single beat before a drop, delete hits for gaps.

    Warp initially off at the source tempo; compare modes when retimingGain envelope shape per beat

  5. Keep a raw version

    Resample the rumble before the sidechain too. A non-pumped rumble loop lets you reshape the pump later with a different curve.

Return tracks can't be frozen, so recording is the reliable way. If you used the duplicated-kick-track method, Freeze → Flatten does the same job in one click.

Recipe C: tuned MIDI rumble

  1. Operator as a sine body

    Algorithm with Oscillator A alone, sine wave. Short amp envelope, tiny pitch envelope for a soft attack.

    Osc A Sin, Level 0 dBAmp env A 2 ms, D 1/8 beat, S −infPitch env +12 st, decay 20 msVoices 1

    Drift works as well: sine shape, filter LP around 200 Hz, voice mode Mono.

  2. Write the MIDI on the root

    Notes around A0–A1 depending on key. Offbeats for bounce, or 16ths 2–3–4 of each beat for a roll. Move the note with your chords.

  3. Same chain, smaller reverb

    The MIDI already provides the rhythm, so the reverb only glues.

    Reverb 0.8–1.5 s, 25–50% wetSaturator Bass ShaperEQ Eight HP 30 / LP 200Compressor sidechain from kick

  4. Blend with Recipe A

    A quiet reverb rumble underneath plus the MIDI rumble on top gives movement and density that still follows harmony.

07

Serum 2

Serum 2 can hold the whole kick-plus-rumble in one patch, which makes it a great instrument for designing your own. Values are starting points. Menu labels can differ slightly between Serum 2 versions, so look for the function if a name doesn't match.

One patch: synthesized kick with its own rumble

  1. Init and set up a mono sine

    Start from the init preset. Oscillator A on a pure sine (basic shapes table, sine position). Other oscillators, noise and sub off for now.

    Osc A sine, Octave −1 or −2Voicing Mono, Legato offGlide 0

  2. Amp envelope = kick body length

    ENV 1 A 0 msH 10–20 msD 180–300 msS 0 / −infR 15 ms

  3. Pitch drop with ENV 2

    Drag ENV 2 onto Osc A coarse pitch. Steep decay curve. This is what makes a sine a kick.

    ENV 2 → Coarse +36 to +48 stA 0D 30–60 msS 0

  4. Click layer

    Noise oscillator with a short transient-type sample, one-shot (not looping), low level. Or skip it and layer a separate top kick in Ableton.

  5. Split dry and rumble with FX busses

    Route Osc A to the main FX and also to an FX bus. Main gets light punch; the bus becomes the rumble.

    Main Distortion (soft/tube) 15–30%Bus Reverb (hall, large, long) Mix 100%→ Distortion 40–70%→ Filter LP 200–260 Hz→ EQ low cut ~30 Hz

  6. Pump it

    Most reliable: do the pump in Ableton with Compressor sidechained from the kick MIDI track (duplicate the patch with bus-only output if needed). In-Serum option: an LFO in envelope/trigger mode, drawn as a drop then a curved rise over about half a beat, modulating the bus output level.

  7. Play the root

    Serum keytracks, so the MIDI note is the tuning. Four-on-the-floor on the note that lands 43–62 Hz (e.g. A1 for A minor with Osc A at the right octave). Confirm with Spectrum in Ableton.

Rolling tuned rumble between the kicks

  1. Body

    Osc A sine, plus Osc B saw quietly underneath for harmonics.

    Osc A sine 0 dBOsc B saw −18 dBVoicing Mono

  2. Filter tracks the note

    Filter LP 24Cutoff 180–250 HzKey track onENV 2 → cutoff small, fast decay

  3. Short notes

    ENV 1 A 2–5 ms, D ≈ 1/16 at your BPM, S 0

  4. The MIDI is the roll

    16ths on steps 2–3–4 of each beat, root note, velocity accenting step 3. Leave step 1 empty for the kick.

  5. FX

    Distortion 30–50%Reverb small, Mix 20–35%Compressor gentleEQ low cut 30 Hz

    Then sidechain from the kick in Ableton, with a faster release (80–120 ms) so each 16th still speaks.

Put a Splice kick inside Serum 2

  1. Load it into a sample oscillator

    Switch an oscillator to its sample mode and drop the kick in. One-shot playback, no loop.

  2. Set its root note

    Measure the kick's settled pitch in Ableton Spectrum first (say G1), then set that as the sample's root. Now playing A1 gives you an A-tuned kick, pitched correctly.

  3. Rumble through the bus

    Same bus chain as the first patch: reverb 100% → distortion → LP filter. You get your favourite sample kick with a rumble you can re-tune by playing a different note.

  4. Experiment: granular drone

    Load the same kick into a granular oscillator, freeze the playhead on the tail, long grains, LP filter low. A sustained rumble tone built from your kick's own texture. Sidechain it hard.

Judge a kick in 20 seconds

Prep it in Ableton

  1. Simpler, one-shot

    Warp off, Snap off, start marker right at the transient.

  2. Shape the length

    Simpler fade-out or amp decay to your target body length (see 05).

  3. Tune to the key

    Spectrum on the settled body, Transpose + Detune until the peak reads the note.

  4. Clean the bottom

    EQ Eight HP at 20–25 Hz, Utility mono. Then feed it into Recipe A.

  5. Once found, use "similar sounds"

    When a kick works, Splice's similar-sounds search usually finds three more that fit the same role.
