    How it works, and where it can be wrong

    Breeder stands on Orbit: its 14 genres, their grooves and basslines, 333 patterns, the generator, Orbit's own variations and the genre fingerprint. The preview is Transplant's audio engine.

    The genes

    A groove is ten gene groups: the kick, the clap, the hats, the percussion (each a two-bar line of 16ths), the bassline (root, fifth, octave, third or seventh of the chord playing, per 16th), the riff's rhythm (rate, steps, gate), the riff's notes (Orbit's pool, order and written sequence, which fit any harmony), the sounds, the key and chords, and the tempo and swing. Children take each group from one parent or the other, sometimes bar 1 from one and bar 2 from the other.

    Mutation and selection

    Mutations stay inside the genre's grammar: the genre's kick anchors always play, the bass stays off the kick where the genre's does, percussion is rewritten as Euclidean rhythms or borrowed from other grooves, and the riff changes with Orbit's own variations. For every slot Breeder draws six candidates and keeps the one with the best mix of genre fit and difference from its siblings, so a generation is good and varied. Drift turns the fit down and lets a family walk out of its genre; the "reads as" label shows where it went.

    Limits

    • The fit scores measure grammar (the riff against Orbit's genre fingerprint, the kick anchors and backbeat, the bass against the kick). They don't measure whether it's a hit. That's what your stars are for.
    • Drums and bass are two-bar lines on a 16th grid; the riff can be longer (up to Orbit's four bars). Triplet and 32nd drum detail belong in Live.
    • "Reads as" compares the riff, the drums and the tempo with Orbit's 14 genres. Acid and techno share a groove, so an acid line can read as techno.
    • The preview plays Orbit's synths and a synthesized kit; your own instruments in Live are the real thing.
