Section 01 · interactive

Melting pot

Four slots. Load a block into each from the banks below — or from the generators — and hear them against each other over a kick. This is where the value is: almost any single block is fine, and almost every interesting groove is two blocks that disagree about where the beat is.

The rule that makes a melting pot work. Two parts in the same rhythmic family cancel each other out — two rolling 16th lines is just one thicker rolling 16th line. Pair by contrast: something driving with something skipping, something rolling with something floating. The motion tag on every block below exists so you can do that deliberately instead of by accident. Surprise me picks four blocks with four different motion types.

Watch the loop-length readout. The pot runs for however long it takes every slot to come home together — pair a 5-bar drift with an 8-bar energy shape and you get a 40-bar phrase that never repeats identically. That is the cheapest hypnotic structure available, and the MIDI export writes out the whole cycle.

Section 02 · interactive

Generators

Three engines that produce more blocks than the bank ever could. Every one of them writes straight into a melting-pot slot.

Why these three. Almost every rhythm in electronic music is one of them. Phasing is a constant spacing that does not divide the bar, so the figure walks. Euclidean is a number of hits spread as evenly as possible across a number of steps, which is how most West African and Latin bell patterns are built. Additive is uneven groupings of a constant pulse — 3+3+2 is the tresillo underneath half of Latin house, and 3+3+3+3+2+2 is a lot of Balkan and psytrance. Between them they cover most of what a sequencer can usefully do.

The bank

Filter by genre, by the motion you want, or both. Every block plays, loads into a slot, and exports as MIDI.

Bass

Bass

The part that decides whether a track drives or rolls. Everything here is in the current key, one or two bars.

Sequence

Sequences & arps

The upper-register motor. Trance and psytrance live almost entirely here.

Stab

Stabs & chord rhythms

Harmony used as a percussion part. Where it lands matters more than what it is.

Energy

Energy shapes

Four- and eight-bar blocks that change across their length instead of looping flat. These are the answer to "it doesn't go anywhere" — drop one in and the loop stops being a loop.

Fills

Fills & turnarounds

One-bar figures for the end of an eight- or sixteen-bar phrase. Use one every eight bars and the track breathes; use one every two and it sounds nervous.

How to build a melting pot

  • One anchor. Something firmly on the grid so the listener can still find beat one. Usually the kick, sometimes the bass.
  • One motor. The part that supplies continuous motion — rolling 16ths, an arp, a shaker.
  • One argument. Something that disagrees: skipping, off-grid, or in a long cycle. This is where the character comes from.
  • One rest. Something sparse and floating that gives the ear a gap. Most amateur productions skip this one and end up dense but static.
  • Four parts, four jobs. If two parts have the same job, delete one.

Making energy fluctuate

  • Use the energy shapes as written, not as loops. They are 4 and 8 bars long because that is the unit at which a listener notices change.
  • Displacement is the cheapest trick here — the same figure moved one 16th later each bar takes four bars to come home and sounds composed rather than looped.
  • Density and register are separate levers. Adding notes and going up an octave both raise energy, but only one of them costs you space in the mix.
  • Alternate: build for eight bars, drop for two, build for six. A straight climb is exhausting; a climb with dips is a track.
  • Gate length is the quietest energy control there is. Same notes, gate down from 90% to 40% across eight bars, and the loop tightens without anything entering or leaving.

A bank is not a substitute for taste. Sixty-six blocks and three generators can produce more combinations than you will ever use, which makes the scarce thing the ability to throw most of them away. Pick four, live with them for eight bars, and delete the one you notice least.
