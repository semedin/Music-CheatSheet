# Wow — the bassline cheat sheet

Wow is a cheat sheet you can play. It holds 35 bassline lanes for house and techno, 20 from the floors of 2025–26 and 15 classics and revivals those ideas came from, with 119 written basslines between them, 48 voices, and a bank of the 24 rhythms that basslines are built from. It writes each idea **six ways**, one for every section of a track, and does the same for a loop you draw yourself. Open `wow.html`; everything runs locally in one standalone file.

The page is built on one claim: **a bassline that works in the drop should not be rewritten for the rest of the track, only edited.** The rhythm is what people dance to, so every section keeps the rhythm and changes one other thing.

Filter the grid by family (house, techno) or by era (right now, classics and revivals). **Surprise me** picks a lane and an idea at random. The years are rough markers for when each idea was everywhere, not a discography. 2-step and Electro 808 play over a broken kick instead of four-on-the-floor.

Each lane has three to six written ideas, a one-line statement of the idea, three reasons it moves a floor, a four-step sound recipe for your own synth, and a few artists whose records sit in that lane. The artist names are a pointer to what to listen to; no pattern here is transcribed from a record.

### The loop

An idea is two bars of 16ths, written as 32 tokens: `.` rest, `-` hold, a number for semitones above the root, and the flags `!` accent, `~` slide into this note, `w` wow, `'` short, `g` ghost (quiet, short and dull). The page shows the tokens for the idea you are hearing.

Every loop obeys a grammar that the tests check: notes never overlap, a slide needs a touching note to slide from, the loop stays between a fifth below the root and an octave and a fifth above it, and in the nineteen **kick-free** lanes no note starts on a kick. The other sixteen land on the kick on purpose (afro, organ, deep house, Italo, acid, EBM and others), and the page counts the notes that do.

**New idea** writes a loop in the lane's own grammar: it measures where the three written ideas put their notes (per 16th position), their note lengths and their note pool, generates bar one from those odds, then repeats it as bar two with a new last beat. It is seeded, so idea #7 is always the same loop.

## The pattern bank

Twenty-four rhythms, each written on the root with at most an octave, a fifth and a ♭3: offbeat, double offbeat, offbeat with octave and ghost, rolling eighths, rolling 16ths, kick and three, stab and sustain, gliding legato, tresillo, tumbao, octave eighths, offbeat octave pop, question and answer, ghost pickups, pedal and ping, push into the one, three against four, chromatic roll, two hits, sub drop, hold and pump, two-step skip, machine sixteenths and hocket with the kick.

Click one and it becomes **your loop** on the lane you are in. It keeps the lane's sound, drums, tempo and fills, so the same rhythm can be heard as tech house, as hard techno or as dub. If the rhythm lands on the kick, the lane stops dodging the kick for that loop and the page counts the hits.

### Where the vocabulary comes from

You asked for ideas sourced from tutorials and video. This session could search the web but could not open pages or watch video, so the sources below were read as search summaries only. They were used to check the names, the common advice and which lanes count as current. No pattern is copied from them, and none is transcribed from a record.

- [The Producer School: 5 Tech House Bass Patterns](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/5-tech-house-bass-patterns-every-producer-needs-to-master): syncopated offbeat, rolling eighths, ghost notes (quiet, short, duller), slides, stab-and-sustain.
- [The Producer School: Master Modern Tech House Bass Lines](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/master-modern-tech-house-bass-lines-complete-tutorial-guide): the offbeat variation with an octave on the first hit and a quieter note in between.
- [The Producer School: Building a Rolling Bassline](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/building-a-rolling-bassline-like-chris-stussy-the-3-note-pattern-that-defines-modern-tech-house): rolling 16ths with the offbeat notes longest and a velocity hierarchy.
- [The Producer School: 5 Essential Bass Patterns That Define Hard House and Trance](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/5-essential-bass-patterns-that-define-hard-house-and-trance-music): offbeat donk, double offbeat, 16th roll, octave jump.
- [Attack Magazine: Warehouse Rolling Techno Bass](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/warehouse-rolling-techno-bass/): layering, fast envelopes, sidechain for separation.
- [Production Music Live: How to make afro tech](https://www.productionmusiclive.com/blogs/news/how-to-make-afro-tech): sub, mid bass and sub drop as layers; tune the kick to the root.
- [Native Instruments: Afro house production 101](https://www.native-instruments.com/blogs/blog/afro-house-production-101): syncopation against the four-on-the-floor.
- [Wikipedia: Tresillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tresillo_(rhythm)): the 3-3-2 cell and the anticipated bass.
- [Beatportal: 2026 mid-year review](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/1489584-the-top-selling-tracks-artists-and-labels-of-2026-mid-year-review): tech house leading, melodic house and techno climbing, hard and psy techno at peak time.

What that reading changed in the page: ghost notes (a new `g` flag, 35% velocity, and velocity now opens the filter, so ghosts are duller as well as quieter), the accent shapes, the tuned kick, the psy-techno lane, thirteen extra ideas in the busiest lanes, and the bank itself.

## Write your own

The editor under the lane title shows the loop as a grid: 32 steps across, pitch from a fourth below the root to an octave and a minor third above, kick columns shaded. Click an empty square to add a note; click a note to apply the selected tool (add/remove, longer, shorter, short, accent, ghost, wow, slide). Any idea can be edited; the first edit copies it into **Your loop**, which then gets the same six sections, exports and options as a written idea, and stays with you when you change lane.

Every edit is normalised: notes are kept monophonic and in range, and a slide survives only where there is a touching note to slide from. The tests feed the normaliser 2,000 random note lists and check each result against the grammar and through all six sections.

## Six versions, one idea

| Section | Bars | What happens to the loop |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 8 | The rhythm skeleton (accents and long notes, a little under half the loop) on the root only. Filter mostly closed. No sub until bar 5, so the outgoing track keeps the low end. |
| Drop 1 | 16 | The loop as written, following the lane's chord roots if it has them. Bar 4 lifts its last beat an octave; bar 8 gets the lane's fill. Played twice. |
| Break | 8 | No kick. One of three treatments: the top layer alone with no sub (*tease*), four long notes on the chord roots (*pad*), or one held root with the filter breathing (*drone*). The last beat is silent. |
| Build | 8 | The full rhythm on one note for four bars, the real loop for two, every 16th for one, then a climb (root, ♭3, 5, octave) with no sub and no kick, and two beats of silence. The filter opens the whole way. |
| Drop 2 | 16 | The same rhythm, developed: the last note answers differently on each pass, the end of every second pass jumps an octave, ghost 16ths lead into long and accented notes, every long note opens up, and there are two different fills. Filter 20% more open, more drive, a ride. Played twice. |
| Outro | 8 | The rhythm on one note, then the skeleton. Filter closes, sub out for the last two bars, kick to the end. |

Together that is a 64-bar sketch of a track. Real arrangements are longer; double the sections in your DAW.

The fills are: a gap, octave pops, a four-note run, a stutter, and a slide from the octave to the root. Hypnotic loop has none on purpose, and drifts its filter over eight bars instead.

## Options

Every lane starts with its own settings, and all of them can be changed:

- **Sound**: any lane can play through any of 48 voices: the 35 lane voices plus thirteen that belong to no lane (wide reese, FM growl, a talker that says *yoy*, 16th wobble, square sub, hollow organ square, short pluck, tape sub, metal knock, bright saw stack, rubber band, sub drop, square acid). The recipe on the page still describes the lane's original sound, and says so.
- **Chords**: the lane's own movement, stay on the root, or one of five two- and four-chord movements. The loop is transposed to each root for two bars.
- **Break**: tease, pad or drone, as in the table above.
- **Fill**: gap, octave pops, run, stutter, slide, or none.
- **Accents**: as written, or a velocity hierarchy by position in the beat: offbeat push, rolling (beat, and, fillers), late push, or flat. Velocity also opens the filter.
- **Tune kick to root**: on by default. The preview kick's fundamental follows the root you choose, so kick and bass agree. Off, each lane uses its own kick pitch.
- **Swing, Length, Pump, Space**: how late the off 16ths land, note length, sidechain depth relative to the lane's own, and the amount of stereo spread and echo.

The tests run every lane through every combination of chords, break and fill and check the result against the same rules.

## Crate and share links

**Save this setup** stores the lane, loop, sound and every setting in a crate in this browser (up to 40). **Copy a link** puts the same setup in the address, so it opens anywhere the page is hosted. The link is read once and then removed from the address bar.

## The preview synth

One mono voice per lane: up to three oscillators, optional two-operator FM (organ click, donk), optional pitch envelope (the mainstage "bwomp", the hard groove tom), drive, a 24 dB low-pass with its own envelope, a clean sine sub, and a sidechain that ducks under every kick. Peak-time rumble adds the kick's own reverb tail, low-passed at 105 Hz and ducked. Some voices add **space**: a short stereo spread and a dotted-8th echo, both fed only from above 300 Hz, so the low end stays mono and dry. With Space at zero the output is mono.

**The wow.** A note flagged `w` opens and closes the filter across its length and raises the resonance. In the five voices with a formant filter (rubber roller, mainstage menace, speed garage warp, melodic techno giant, rave hoover), three band-pass filters also move through vowel positions, so the note says *wow*, *ow*, *weow*, *waow* or *whoa*. Speed garage warp adds a filter wobble on 8ths under every long note. The **Wow** knob scales all of it.

Slides are real glides: the voice is legato, so a slid note bends from the previous pitch without retriggering the envelope.

This is a browser synth for judging notes, rhythm and movement. It is not a finished patch. The recipe on the page tells you what to build in Serum, Wavetable, Operator or whatever you use.

## Exports

- **MIDI, this section** (8 bars) or **the whole track** (64 bars). Format 1: a conductor with tempo and a marker at each section, the bass, and optionally the drums on channel 10 (GM notes). CC74 carries the section's filter setting bar by bar; CC1 rises and falls across each wow note. A slide is written as a note that overlaps the one before it, so use a mono, legato patch with glide. Ghost notes are low velocity, accents high.
- **WAV**, 16-bit stereo at 44.1 kHz, cut exactly on the bar line so a section loops. Untick *Include drums* for the bass alone (the sidechain pump stays in).
- MIDI is on a straight grid. The preview swings the 16ths in the lanes that need it; the page tells you by how much when you export.

Settings are remembered in this browser.

## What is checked, and what is not

`node wow-src/test.mjs` checks the engine with no browser: all 119 written ideas and 300 generated ideas per lane against the grammar; all 24 bank patterns on every lane in every section; ghost notes and accent shapes; the editor's normaliser; that no two written ideas are the same; every combination of the options; every section of every idea for overlaps, register and slides; that kick-free lanes never start a note on a kick that is actually playing; that each section does what the table above says (root-only intro, silent beats, the climb, sub and kick in and out); that the energy readout puts drop 2 at or above drop 1, and drop 1 above the intro, the break and the outro; and that the MIDI files are well formed with every note-on closed.

`node wow-src/browser-check.mjs` drives the page in Chromium: playback, switching lanes and sections while playing, the whole-track mode, exports, a reload and a phone-width layout. It adds, changes and removes notes in the editor, stamps a bank pattern, saves to the crate and opens a share link. It renders the drop and the break of all 35 lanes offline and checks each is finite, audible and below full scale. It also checks that turning Wow up makes a talking lane measurably brighter, that the Sub knob adds level, that a swapped voice is playing within a beat, and that Space adds stereo width and is mono at zero.

Those are checks of rules and signal integrity. The readouts on the page (notes per bar, notes on a kick, how much of the time the bass sounds, gestures, the root in Hz) count things. **None of this measures whether a bassline is good, and nothing here can promise a chart position.** The lanes, the tempos and the reasons given are a producer's reading of current club music, not a statistical study of the charts. Trust your ears and a loud system.

### Limits

- No native Serum, Ableton or other presets are exported. The recipes are steps to follow.
- The research was search summaries, as described above. Nobody watched a video or read a chart page in full.
- The editor is click-only: no drag, no keyboard entry, and one loop at a time.
- A borrowed voice keeps its own level and settings, so some combinations are louder or duller than others. Use Tone and Drive.
- Idea names are descriptions. No pattern is a transcription of a record.
- The preview has a compressor and a soft limiter so it is safe to listen to. It is not a master.
- The energy bar is a heuristic (note density, filter, sub, kick), there to show the shape of the track.
- Laptop speakers reproduce very little below 100 Hz. Use headphones or monitors.
