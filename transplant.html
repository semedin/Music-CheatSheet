Fieldwork · Arrange · from 8 bars to a track

TransplantYour loop. Their arrangement.

Stuck on the 8-bar loop? Borrow the arrangement of a track you love. Transplant measures where every part of the reference comes in and out and every move in its transitions, then rebuilds all of it on your own parts: your kick drops out where theirs does, the roll lands on your clap, the filter sweep opens your riff, the throw catches your hit. Then it measures how close the shape is, and hands it to Ableton.

    How it works, and where it can be wrong

    Transplant runs X-Ray's and Blueprint's engines on the reference, in this page, and Orbit's patches for the preview.

    Reading the reference

    Blueprint measures seven lanes per beat (kick, clap, hats, sustained highs, bass, chords and synths, noise) and every move in the transitions. Transplant reads each lane per bar and finds the cheapest on/off path through it, where switching costs least on an 8-bar line and most on an odd bar (a Viterbi segmentation), so parts come and go the way an arranger draws them. Filter curves are smoothed into a single direction (isotonic regression) and reduced to the fewest breakpoints that redraw them within a sixth of an octave.

    Rebuilding it on yours

    Each of your parts follows a lane. Parts sharing a lane are stacked: the hook first, then the chords, then the pad (the pad first in breaks and intros); each layer enters on a phrase line where the reference's lane is fuller, measured by its level and its tension, ranked across the phrases. Rolls are snapped to the subdivision the reference's roll plays at that moment; stutters repeat your own first slice; a throw echoes the hit that's actually there; a gap cuts every note and the sends.

    Limits

    • A lead and the chords under it can't be told apart in a full mix, so the reference has one lane for both. Which of your parts comes first in that lane is a convention you can change per part.
    • The reference's lanes are read from audio: a part that plays quietly under a busy mix can read as absent. Check the lanes against your ears, and paint bars on or off.
    • Constant tempo is assumed, as in X-Ray. Your loop is played at your tempo; the A/B jumps to the same bar of the reference at its own tempo.
    • Filter sweeps arrive in Live as CC74 on each part's clip, which a synth can MIDI-learn; Auto Filter on a group needs the breakpoints drawn as automation (the plan lists them).
    • The preview is a sketch of your parts with Orbit's synths. Your own instruments, in Live, are the real thing. Measurements count notes, bars and lanes; they don't measure taste.
