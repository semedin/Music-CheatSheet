        03 / EIGHT RULES
        What all thirty-four have in common.
        
  
          
    
        1. Know where the kick is. Either never start a note on it, or land on it on purpose and let the sidechain separate them. Half-decisions make mud.
          2. 
    
        2. Two bars: a question, then an answer. Bar two repeats bar one and changes only the last beat. That is enough to loop for five minutes.
          3. 
    
        3. Rests are notes. The gap before a hit is what makes it heavy. If the loop feels weak, delete something.
          4. 
    
        4. Length is groove. Short, short, long. Change the note lengths before you change the pitches.
          5. 
    
        5. One gesture per bar. One slide, one wow, one octave jump. A bassline with a single feature is a hook; with five it is a solo.
          6. 
    
        6. Put the root where the system is. Roots between F and A (44–55 Hz) hit hardest on most club rigs. Check the Hz readout when you change key.
          7. 
    
        7. Change one thing per section. Rhythm in the intro, notes in the drop, development in drop two. Keep the rhythm and people keep dancing.
          8. 
    
        8. The silence is part of the bassline. Pull the sub a bar before the drop and stop everything for two beats. The drop is only as big as the hole in front of it.
          9. 
  
        
  
        These are patterns and starting sounds, not finished records. The readouts count notes and time; they do not measure taste, and nothing here can promise a chart position. Read the notes in WOW.md for what is and is not checked.
