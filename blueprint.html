The sections, and why each is called that

Blueprint uses six section types and nothing else. Each has one definition. They are listed in song order; the rules are tried by their number (Drop first, Groove last) and the first that fits names the section. Neighbouring sections of the same type count as one; a change inside them shows up in the phrases. Numbers only count repeats: Drop 2 is the second drop.

    Lining it up in Live

      How it works, and where it can be wrong

      Blueprint runs on X-Ray's engine for tempo, grid and loudness, and adds its own analysis on top. Everything runs in this page. Each detector is tested against a synthesized track whose moves are known, and against the same track with no moves at all.

      Seeing what was filtered

      The spectrum is read in semitone-wide bands and split into its sustained part (pads, synths, bass, risers) and its hits (drums). Above 1.2 kHz the split runs on a 23 ms window so dense hats count as hits. A filter sweep is first spotted as a spectral edge moving steadily, then confirmed beat by beat: each beat is compared with the same beat of the loop once the filter is open, and fitted with a filter skirt. The cutoff you see is that fit, smoothed over five beats. Checking the music and the drums separately says what the filter is on.

      Everything else

      Risers: a steady climb in a noise-like layer (flat inside each band). Pitch risers and downlifters: a tonal peak that glides, semitone by semitone, instead of stepping. Tape stops: the whole spectrum sliding down frame after frame. Rolls: hits filling a subdivision grid. Delay throws: a chain of synced, steadily decaying repeats after a cut. Gaps, stutters, crashes, booms, reverse swells, sidechain, width and fades each have a measured shape, and a section is only cut where the parts or the moves say so.

      Limits

      • A lead line and the chords under it can't be told apart in a full mix, so they share one lane.
      • Sidechain pumping is read where nothing else hits on the beat. If a lead or stab plays on every kick, the pump is hidden and isn't reported.
      • The spectrum is read up to 10.5 kHz, so a low-pass counts as open once its cutoff passes about 8 kHz.
      • Cutoffs are where the filter's slope begins, read through the rest of the mix: expect them within about half an octave, and a high-pass under a busy build can read higher.
      • A static filter on a section is reported only when its skirt holds against similar sections. A section that is simply darker by arrangement can't be told from a filtered one.
      • Constant tempo is assumed, as in X-Ray. Check the grid with the click before trusting the rest.
